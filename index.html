About this estimate

Auckland Transport buses and trains carry automatic passenger counters and publish a broad live occupancy category. AT's counters switch category at 40% and 90% of seated capacity, so each category corresponds to a known band of real loads. This page combines those bands with vehicle capacities to estimate how many people are aboard right now. It is an estimate, not a count.

Where a vehicle sits inside its band is set once for the whole network. Normally each vehicle is placed at the 30th percentile of its own band: every trip starts and ends empty, and service is sized for the peak, so more vehicles sit near the bottom of a band than the top. Overnight and in the early morning, when only a fraction of the fleet is out and those vehicles run close to empty, the position drops to the 15th percentile. Everything in between — weekday peak, weekday midday and weekends alike — is treated the same, because a bus reporting "many seats" on a Saturday afternoon is not meaningfully different from one reporting it at 8am. Buses are assumed to be XLBs (47 seated, 78 total). Double-deckers, identified by fleet number (HE05xx, NB50xx, NB53xx, NB54xx) or by running on a route operated entirely by double-deckers (NX1, NX2, WX1), are assumed to seat 85 with 110 total. Trains are assumed to be 3-car units: 230 seated, 143 standing.

The range is a 90% interval. Each vehicle's true load is modelled as a triangular distribution across its category band, and the variances are summed across vehicles — so the aggregate is much tighter than the raw bounds, because independent errors cancel. It captures uncertainty within the reported categories only. It does not cover systematic error: the capacity assumptions, double-decker identification by fleet number, or the accuracy of the counters themselves. Those shift the whole figure together and are not something this page can measure.
